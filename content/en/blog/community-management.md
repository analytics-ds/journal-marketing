**Community management** refers to the set of actions carried out to build, grow and moderate a brand's presence on social media. Led by a community manager, this role combines content creation, relationship with followers and tracking the impact of each post, in service of marketing objectives defined upstream.

## What is community management?

Community management covers the daily handling of a brand's social accounts: publishing content, replying to comments and direct messages, monitoring brand mentions and engaging with the community of followers built around it. This definition goes beyond simply posting, since it also includes reputation monitoring and relaying signals expressed by customers on social media.

The term is sometimes used interchangeably with **social media manager**, though a distinction exists in some organizations. A community manager focuses mainly on daily engagement and moderation, while a social media manager carries the broader editorial and advertising strategy across channels. In smaller structures, both roles are most often carried by the same person.

## The main missions of a community manager

A community manager's missions generally fall into three areas. The first is **content creation**: writing posts, producing visuals or short videos, adapting the same message to the formats specific to each social platform.

The second area is community engagement, which covers moderating comments, replying to direct messages and running occasional actions such as polls or highlighting content posted spontaneously by followers. This activity ties directly into [user generated content](/en/blog/ugc-marketing/), which a community manager must know how to spot and showcase without distorting the customer's voice.

The third area is monitoring: tracking brand mentions, conversation trends specific to each network and competitor posts, to adjust the editorial line continuously rather than fixing it for an entire quarter.

## The essential skills for this profession

### Technical skills

A community manager knows the technical specifics of each platform: accepted publishing formats, scheduling best practices, reading native analytics. Understanding how a recommendation algorithm works, such as the [TikTok algorithm](/en/blog/tiktok-algorithm/), directly shapes the reach a post can get and therefore guides format and timing choices.

### Human and writing skills

Beyond technical knowledge, the role requires fast writing skills suited to short formats, along with the interpersonal ease needed to handle sensitive situations, negative reviews or an early sign of backlash, without alienating the community. This human dimension also explains why some brands work with [nano influencers](/en/blog/nano-influencer/) close to their community to relay certain messages with more credibility than a brand account.

## The tools and platforms of community management

A community manager relies daily on a scheduling tool to plan posts across several accounts at once, a monitoring tool to track brand mentions beyond its own channels, and the native analytics provided by each platform to measure the real performance of posts. Which platforms get followed depends directly on the target audience: a consumer brand will favor Instagram and TikTok, while a B2B company will concentrate most of its activity on LinkedIn.

This use of tools remains a means, not an end: a well filled analytics dashboard only has value if it informs concrete decisions about what content to produce or how often to post on each channel.

## In house or agency community management: which to choose?

Companies hand community management either to an in house team or to a specialized agency, a choice that mainly depends on the number of accounts to manage and the availability of dedicated skills internally. An in house team knows the products and company culture better, while an agency brings experience gained across several brands and the capacity to absorb occasional spikes in activity.

This organizational choice does not remove the same legal obligations: as soon as a paid collaboration is set up with a content creator, the rules set by [influencer law](/en/blog/influencer-law-france/) apply, whether the brand manages this relationship directly or through an agency.

## Training, salary and career progression

Several paths lead to the community manager profession: specialized digital communication programs, business or communication schools, or a career change backed by hands on social media experience gained independently. Hiring relies as much on a portfolio of managed accounts or created content as on a specific degree title.

Salary varies significantly depending on experience, industry and the size of the employer, without a single figure being able to reliably summarize this market. Career progression frequently leads to social media lead, digital project manager or content strategy consultant positions, once solid hands on experience has been built.

## Community management and marketing strategy: what business impact?

Community management is not limited to an execution function: when run well, it feeds the overall marketing strategy, by relaying direct signals about how products, campaigns and customer service are perceived. The comments and messages received daily are raw material that few other channels provide with the same immediacy.

This role within the strategy explains why community management benefits from being steered in line with the company's other marketing levers, rather than treated as an isolated channel left at the edge of the overall strategy. A community manager who regularly shares field observations with product and communication teams turns a role often seen as operational into a genuine listening post for the brand.
