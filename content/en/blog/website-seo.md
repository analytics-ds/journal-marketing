Website SEO refers to the set of techniques that improve a page's position in the organic results of search engines. It is no longer limited to Google: generative engines like ChatGPT or Perplexity also draw on website content to build their answers. A properly optimized site attracts qualified traffic without depending solely on paid campaigns, which makes it a durable acquisition lever for any online activity.

## What is website SEO

Website SEO, also called Search Engine Optimization, groups together the actions aimed at improving a site's visibility in search engine results pages, as opposed to the paid advertising results that fall under SEA. The end goal remains to position strategic pages for the queries the target audience actually types, rather than chasing high volume keywords unrelated to the site's activity.

This discipline rests on three inseparable pillars. The **technical** pillar conditions the ability of indexing robots to crawl and understand the site. The **content** pillar covers the semantic relevance and editorial quality of pages. The **popularity** pillar measures the authority gained through inbound links. Neglecting any one of these pillars mechanically limits the results of the other two, even when they are perfectly handled otherwise.

A brochure site, an online store and an editorial blog do not share the same priorities across these three pillars, but none of them can afford to ignore any of them. An e-commerce store with several thousand product pages puts more weight on the technical pillar, where a crawling error can deindex entire categories within days. An editorial site instead relies more heavily on the content pillar, where semantic coverage and publishing consistency drive most of the organic traffic.

## The technical foundations of solid SEO

Technical SEO is the foundation everything else rests on: excellent content published on a site that search engines cannot properly crawl remains invisible, no matter how good it is.

### Loading speed and mobile adaptation

Loading speed directly influences ranking and the reading experience. A valid HTTPS certificate, compressed images and lightweight code all reduce page display time. Mobile adaptation is no longer optional either: Google indexes and ranks sites based primarily on their mobile version, which makes a responsive layout essential for any newly published page.

### Architecture, crawling and indexing

A readable site architecture places every important page within three or four clicks of the homepage, with short, descriptive URLs rather than opaque numeric identifiers. The robots.txt file and the XML sitemap guide crawlers toward the pages to prioritize, while a regular [SEO audit](/en/blog/seo-audit/) helps spot pages mistakenly blocked or redirect chains that dilute the popularity passed between pages. A [dedicated SEO checklist for website redesigns](/en/blog/seo-website-redesign-checklist/) becomes essential as soon as a migration or a URL structure change is planned, to avoid losing the SEO work already done.

## Content and semantics in the service of SEO

Content answers the search intent behind each targeted query. An article that defines a term does not follow the same structure as a comparison or a tutorial, and confusing these intents produces pages that satisfy neither the reader nor the ranking algorithm.

Writing careful [title tags and meta descriptions](/en/blog/title-tag-meta-description/) shapes the click through rate from search results, even before the page content itself is read. A rich semantic field, built from synonyms and questions related to the topic, helps search engines understand a page's exact scope and prevents it from competing against another page on the same site for the same query. Internal linking, which connects pages to each other through explicit anchor text, completes this work by distributing popularity toward strategic pages.

## Link building and site popularity

Inbound links remain a major trust signal for search engines: a link from a recognized site in the same industry carries more weight than a generic link with no thematic connection. Link building consists of earning this kind of link progressively, favoring relevance and the diversity of referring domains over raw volume.

Certain practices instead damage a site's popularity: buying links in bulk from low quality site networks, or repeating the same over optimized anchor text across dozens of links, rank among the most costly [SEO mistakes to avoid](/en/blog/seo-mistakes-to-avoid/). A natural link profile mixes anchor types, comes from varied domains and builds up over time rather than within a few weeks.

## SEO facing generative engines

Website SEO is no longer limited to classic Google results. AI Overviews and answers from ChatGPT, Perplexity or Copilot rely on already indexed content to build their responses, and a site absent from these answers loses a growing source of visibility. This is the field of [GEO, or Generative Engine Optimization](/en/blog/what-is-geo-guide/), which extends the fundamentals of classic SEO.

In practice, this means checking that generative engine crawlers, such as GPTBot or PerplexityBot, are not mistakenly blocked in the robots.txt file, and structuring content into clear, self contained answers rather than paragraphs that require reading the whole page to be understood. Content that answers a question head on, in a few dense sentences placed right under the matching subheading, has a better chance of being cited by these engines.

## Budget and timeline for effective website SEO

The cost of website SEO varies widely depending on the size of the site, how competitive the sector is and the type of provider chosen. It covers three distinct items: the time spent on analysis and writing, the technical and editorial expertise involved, and the professional tracking and analysis tools.

| Site profile | Indicative monthly budget | Timeline for results |
|---|---|---|
| Small site, low competition | A few hundred euros | 3 to 6 months |
| Growing site, moderately competitive sector | Several hundred to a few thousand euros | 6 to 12 months |
| High traffic site, highly competitive sector | Several thousand euros | 12 to 18 months |

These figures remain indicative: a highly competitive sector demands more resources than a niche market, even for a comparably sized site. An offer promising guaranteed rankings within a few weeks is instead a red flag, since no provider controls the search engines' ranking algorithm.

SEO is best treated as an ongoing investment rather than a one off expense. A steady, modest budget maintained over time generally produces better results than a spike of investment followed by a complete stop, since search engines value publishing and updating consistency at least as much as the initial volume of work.
