**Internal linking** covers every link that connects the pages of a single site to one another. It is one of the most accessible SEO levers to work on, because it depends on no outside party: unlike external link building, it stays entirely under the control of the site's own editor, who alone decides its structure and its anchors.

## What is internal linking in SEO

Internal linking is the organization of the hyperlinks that connect a website's pages to one another. Each link has two parts: the destination page and the **anchor**, the clickable text that introduces it. These two elements shape both what a visitor understands before clicking and what search engines take away about the topic of the targeted page.

This organization is one of the pillars of [website SEO](/en/blog/website-seo/) alongside technical work and content, but it stands apart on one important point: it is entirely controllable from inside the site, without depending on any third party to secure a link.

A well linked site shows a readable hierarchy between its pages: pillar pages that cover a broad topic, and more specific secondary pages that link back up to them. That hierarchy is not a cosmetic detail, it directly shapes how Google crawls the site and understands the relative importance of each page.

## Why internal linking matters for SEO

Well built internal linking serves visitors and search engines at the same time, which makes it a rare lever able to meet two goals at once without trading one off against the other.

### Making navigation easier for visitors

A link placed at the right spot saves a reader from going back or reopening a search to find related content. That continuity of reading lowers the odds that a visitor leaves the site after a single page, by naturally offering the logical next step to what they just read.

### Distributing internal PageRank

Internal links also pass on part of the authority a page has built up to the pages it links to, a mechanism often summed up as **link juice**. A page that receives many external inbound links can redistribute part of that authority toward newer or less visible pages, as long as it is itself well connected to the rest of the site.

### Helping search engines crawl and index the site

Search engine crawlers largely discover a site's pages by following its internal links. A page that no internal link mentions, known as an orphan page, is much harder to crawl and index, even though it genuinely exists on the server. **Google Search Console** flags this exact kind of issue precisely, which makes it a first check before any other analysis.

## Internal linking and the semantic silo: what is the difference

The semantic silo is an internal linking method, popularized in France, that pushes the hierarchy logic further. It organizes pages from the same topic into a closed set, and deliberately limits links leaving that boundary toward other topics on the site. The goal is to concentrate semantic relevance around one specific subject, rather than diluting it across cross topic links.

This method suits sites that cover clearly separate topics well, where each silo can live almost independently. It becomes more restrictive for a general interest outlet like a marketing blog, where an article about measurement tools naturally benefits from linking to a content strategy article, even though the two topics do not belong to the same strict silo. Internal linking remains the general concept, the semantic silo one of its most rigid applications, best kept for structures suited to it.

## Internal linking versus external link building

Internal linking differs from external link building in the nature of the links involved and in how much control a site's editor holds over them.

| Criterion | Internal linking | External link building |
|---|---|---|
| Origin of the links | Pages on the same site | Other domains |
| Editor's control | Full: anchors, pages and number of links chosen freely | Partial: depends on the willingness of the site granting the link |
| Main goal | Structuring the site and guiding navigation | Strengthening the domain's overall authority |
| Effect on PageRank | Redistributing authority already present on the site | Bringing in new authority from outside |

These two levers do not replace one another. A site that earns quality external links but never redistributes that authority internally leaves part of its potential untapped, since the pages best cited from outside pass nothing on to pages they never mention.

## How to set up effective internal linking

Effective internal linking is built step by step, starting with the site's structure before moving down to each individual link.

### Identifying pillar pages and secondary pages

The first step is spotting the pages that cover a broad topic and should concentrate the most internal inbound links, and those that cover a narrower angle and should link back up to the matching pillar page. This mapping naturally emerges during a [technical SEO audit](/en/blog/technical-seo-audit/), which reviews the existing architecture before any fix.

### Choosing relevant link anchors

An effective anchor describes the destination page in clear words, rather than generic phrases like click here or learn more. This clarity helps the reader decide whether to follow the link, and gives search engines an extra clue about the topic of the targeted page.

### Respecting the three click rule

This rule, widely repeated across SEO guides, recommends that no important page sit more than three clicks away from the homepage. A page buried too deep in the site structure mechanically receives fewer internal links and becomes harder to crawl, for search engines and visitors alike.

### Avoiding common internal linking mistakes

The most frequent [SEO mistakes to avoid](/en/blog/seo-mistakes-to-avoid/) on this lever are well known: identical anchors repeated across the whole site, orphan pages that no link mentions, or links placed only in a generic navigation block with no contextual link anywhere in the body text. These three flaws weaken how readable the site's hierarchy is, for a visitor and for a search engine alike.

## Which tools to use to analyze internal linking

A first free check comes from the coverage report in **Google Search Console**, which shows indexed pages and helps spot the ones that visibly receive too few internal links. A dedicated crawling tool goes further by mapping every link on the site, flagging broken links and listing orphan pages that no other page links back to.

This kind of check is best planned over time, rather than run only once. A [site redesign SEO checklist](/en/blog/seo-website-redesign-checklist/) systematically includes a step to verify internal linking, since a redesign is one of the moments when links break most easily, when URLs change without the internal links pointing to them being updated accordingly.
