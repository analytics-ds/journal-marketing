> **In short:**
> 1. GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) is the discipline of optimizing content to be cited by AI engines: AI Overviews, ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini.
> 2. It complements SEO without replacing it: same foundations, but citation criteria differ from ranking factors.
> 3. Academic work (Princeton, Georgia Tech) measures up to 40% additional visibility in generative answers for optimized content.

A growing share of purchase decisions now starts with a question asked to an AI, not with ten blue links. **GEO**, Generative Engine Optimization, is marketers' answer to that shift: making your brand a source generative engines choose to cite.

## The definition of GEO

**Generative Engine Optimization** covers the techniques that increase the probability of content being **picked up, cited and recommended** in AI-generated answers: Google's AI Overviews, ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini or Copilot.

The fundamental nuance versus classic search optimization: you no longer optimize for a ranking algorithm that orders pages, but for **language models that compose an answer** and choose their sources. Ranking first is no longer enough, you have to be quotable.

## GEO and SEO: what actually changes

| Aspect | SEO | GEO |
|--------|-----|-----|
| Goal | Rank in the SERPs | Get cited in AI answers |
| Unit of work | The page | The extractable passage |
| Metrics | Rankings, CTR, traffic | Citations, AI share of voice |
| Key levers | Keywords, backlinks, tech | Structure, authority, verifiable data |
| Starting point | The keyword | The prompt |

The two disciplines share their foundations: a technically sound site, quality content and a solid link profile serve both goals. GEO adds a layer of requirements specific to generative models.

## The four pillars of AI-cited content

### 1. Self-contained passages

Models extract complete blocks of information that make sense out of context: a direct answer under each heading, sharp definitions, lists and tables. This is the core of the method described in our [AI Overviews analysis](/en/blog/ai-overviews-brand-citations/).

### 2. Evidence over claims

Sourced figures, cited studies, precise dates: factual accuracy is a direct selection criterion. It ties into the trust-signal work detailed in E-E-A-T: proving expertise.

### 3. Markup machines understand

Schema.org structured data (Article, FAQPage, Person, Organization) makes extraction reliable. The llms.txt file completes the setup by pointing AI crawlers to key content.

### 4. Measurable authority

Generative engines favor domains recognized in their field. Backlinks, brand mentions and press citations remain the bedrock: a site invisible to Google is invisible to ChatGPT too.

## Where to start: the 5-step method

1. **Map the prompts** where your brand should appear, starting from real customer questions.
2. **Audit the current state**: who is cited today on those prompts, and why.
3. **Optimize or create** content that answers better than the incumbent sources.
4. **Mark up** with Schema.org and publish an llms.txt.
5. **Measure citations** earned in each engine, then iterate monthly.

Each engine has its own source and format preferences, and knowing them tells you where to concentrate your effort depending on your sector. To get support, our [ranking of GEO agencies](/en/blog/best-geo-agencies-ai-overviews/) lists the players with a genuinely structured offer.

<summary>Does GEO replace SEO?</summary>

No. SEO fundamentals remain the prerequisite: sound technical foundations, relevant content, authority. GEO adds an optimization layer specific to generative engines, with its own formats and its own citation metrics.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Which engines does GEO concern?</summary>

Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode, ChatGPT and its web search, Perplexity, Gemini, Microsoft Copilot, and more broadly any assistant that composes web-sourced answers.

</details>

<details>
<summary>How long before GEO delivers results?</summary>

On niche queries, citations can appear within weeks. On competitive topics, expect several months of combined work on content, markup and authority, just like SEO.
