The **TikTok algorithm** does not work like other social platforms: it does not rank videos based on the popularity of the account that posts them, but on how real users behave in front of each piece of content, evaluated individually. Understanding this logic has become essential for any brand or creator seeking visibility on the platform, since the distribution rules differ sharply from those of Instagram or LinkedIn.

## How the TikTok algorithm works in 2026

The For You feed works like a prediction engine: for each video posted, TikTok evaluates whether it deserves to be shown to a wider audience or should remain limited to a few hundred views. This decision is made continuously, video by video, rather than once and for all at the moment of posting.

This mechanism explains why an account with few followers can occasionally reach a massive audience, while an established account can see a video plateau quickly. Account history matters less than the immediate performance of the content being distributed.

### A separate evaluation for every video

Each post triggers its own evaluation cycle, independent of the results obtained by the account's previous videos. An account can therefore post a widely distributed video followed by one that stays confined to a small audience, without this reflecting a technical issue or a lasting penalty. This lack of memory from one piece of content to the next sets TikTok apart from organic reach logics seen on other networks, where an account's engagement history weighs more heavily on the distribution of each new post.

## The distribution phases of a video on the For You feed

A TikTok video goes through several distribution stages before, potentially, reaching a wide audience.

During the initial test phase, which lasts roughly one to three hours, the video is shown to a small panel made up of the account's followers and users active on similar content. If the signals collected are good, distribution expands in successive stages, often over a 24 to 48 hour window.

This is why a TikTok video can keep gaining traction for several days after it is posted, unlike formats whose visibility concentrates almost entirely within the first few hours.

At each stage it clears, the algorithm re-evaluates the signals gathered on a larger sample before deciding whether to extend distribution further or stabilize it. A video that maintains a strong retention rate as its audience grows keeps progressing, while a drop in interest among a wider audience quickly slows the extension of its reach.

## The signals that weigh most in ranking

The algorithm tracks a large number of signals, but they do not all carry the same weight. **Watch time** and completion rate are among the most decisive indicators: a video watched to the end, or replayed, signals content that genuinely holds attention.

On the interaction side, active actions count more than passive ones. A share, particularly sent as a direct message, weighs more than a save, which itself weighs more than a comment, with all three signals generally valued above a simple like. This hierarchy reflects a simple logic: an action that requires more effort from the user says more about the perceived quality of the content.

| Signal type | Example | Relative weight |
|---|---|---|
| Strong active action | Share, direct message | Most decisive |
| Active action | Saving the video | High |
| Moderate active action | Comment | Medium to high |
| Passive action | Like | Weakest |

### What this hierarchy means in practice

Optimizing for likes alone, by relying on an easy hook rather than content that delivers on its promise until the end, rarely produces good results over time. Conversely, content that naturally prompts a comment, a save for later, or a share with a specific person accumulates several strong signals in a single interaction, which explains why some formats that seem less spectacular at first glance sometimes distribute better than more polished videos.

## Hashtags and audio: their real role in recommendation

Hashtags help the algorithm place a video within a specific community or topic, but their effect stays limited if they are too generic. Broad hashtags, used by millions of unrelated videos, provide no useful targeting. More specific hashtags, tied to an identifiable niche, help the algorithm choose the right test audience more effectively.

Audio also plays a role in how a video is indexed and recommended: a sound tied to a trend or an active community can make it easier to connect with users already engaged with that type of content.

### Native vertical format still matters

Beyond hashtags and audio, how a video is built directly affects its ability to hold attention. Vertical framing designed for the phone screen, a hook placed within the very first seconds, and editing that avoids dead time all favor a better completion rate, which, as seen above, remains one of the most decisive signals. A video simply cropped from a horizontal format, without adapting its pacing or construction, generally starts at a disadvantage on this criterion.

## Does follower count still matter on TikTok

Unlike platforms where organic reach depends heavily on the size of an existing audience, TikTok evaluates each post independently of the account's follower count. A recent account with a small audience can see a video shown to a very large public if the early signals are favorable.

This trait explains the space that emerging creators continue to occupy on the platform, several years after its launch. It also implies that an audience built on other channels does not automatically guarantee good distribution on TikTok: every video starts, in a sense, from zero in front of the algorithm.

### Posting consistency, an indirect factor

Posting with some regularity nonetheless helps the algorithm better identify an account's topic and the audience its content speaks to, which can refine the test panel targeted for each new post. This effect stays indirect: it makes the algorithm's job easier without replacing the intrinsic quality of each video, which remains the truly decisive factor for its distribution.

## What this logic changes for a brand content strategy

For a brand, this mechanism shifts the center of gravity of the strategy: posting frequency and community size matter less than each video's individual ability to hold attention until the end. A [content strategy](/en/blog/content-marketing/) designed for TikTok must therefore accept that content formatted as a plain ad repurpose rarely performs as well as a format native to the platform.

This also means better understanding the target audience before producing content: building a precise [marketing persona](/en/blog/create-marketing-persona/) helps identify the codes, tone and formats that hold that specific audience's attention, rather than reusing codes designed for other networks.

When a brand works with creators to carry this type of content, the transparency obligations specific to commercial influence, governed by [influencer law](/en/blog/influencer-law-france/), apply just as they do on other social platforms.
