The **price of web writing** varies significantly depending on the billing method chosen, per word, hourly or per day, and on the experience of the writer hired. Understanding these different pricing methods makes it possible to set a realistic budget, whether the goal is to pay for content or to evaluate a quote received.

## What are the average web writing rates in 2026

Rates observed in web writing cover a wide range. Per word, prices go from 0.02 to 0.25 euro depending on the writer's experience and specialization, with a beginner rate around 0.03 to 0.05 euro and an experienced rate reaching up to 0.15 euro. Per project, a short 500 word piece sits between 120 and 400 euros, a 1000 word piece between 100 and 250 euros for a standard piece, and a long, well researched piece can exceed 500 euros. Per day, the average daily rate most often sits between 370 and 600 euros.

These gaps reflect the diversity of profiles on the market: a generalist beginner writer, an SEO web writer specialized in a technical **niche**, or a conversion focused copywriter. The rate also depends on the type of content requested: an informational blog post is not billed like a product page or a sales page, which demand denser persuasion work for a often smaller word count.

## Per word, hourly or per project billing: what is the difference

**Per word billing** pays for each word written, sometimes counted in characters or in standard pages. It is the most common method on writing platforms, but also the most criticized by experienced writers: it ignores the time spent on keyword research, content structuring or exchanges with the client, and mechanically pushes rates down through a volume driven logic.

**Hourly billing** stays close to this same quantity driven logic, with the added downside of quickly capping the achievable rate: a client then reasons in billed hours rather than in value delivered, which limits the writer's room to negotiate.

The **average daily rate** consists of setting a price for a full day of work rather than for a quantity of words or hours. It is the method most often recommended by experienced writers: it is easy for the client to understand, it leaves room to estimate the time actually needed for a task, and it better values the underlying work, research, briefing, iterations, that does not show up in the final text. It applies just as well to a blog post as to a sales page or an email sequence.

## The average daily rate of a freelance web writer

The **average daily rate** observed among freelance web writers most often sits between 370 and 600 euros per day. This price in principle covers a complete service: research, outline structuring, writing, proofreading and one round of revisions with the client.

This daily rate remains distinct from the writer's net income, which still has to account for social contributions, taxes, tools and the unbillable time spent on prospecting or admin work. A displayed day rate of 500 euros does not therefore translate directly into an equivalent income, a nuance often missing from pricing tables found online.

## What factors make the price of a blog post vary

Several elements explain the price gap between two seemingly similar web writing services.

**The writer's experience** plays a direct role: a beginner writer necessarily charges less than an established one, who has developed expertise and the ability to deliver usable content without excessive back and forth.

**The area of expertise** also weighs heavily on the calculation. A generalist piece requires less research than a technical, legal or medical piece, which involves understanding a complex topic before being able to explain it clearly to a non specialist reader.

**The type of content** also changes the budget: an informational blog post, a short conversion focused product page, and a long sales page do not require the same amount of work or the same copywriting expertise.

**The expected quality of the brief** finally influences the final rate. A poorly structured brief, without a clear editorial line or defined goal, forces the writer to ask questions and make assumptions, which lengthens the job and justifies a higher rate than a complete brief provided upfront.

**Add on skills** brought by the writer also tip the scale. A plain writing job is not billed the same as a service that includes keyword research, on page SEO optimization, writing the title tag and meta description, or thinking through the internal linking to plan. A writer able to handle this technical layer, on top of the writing itself, justifies a higher rate than one who delivers a plain text only.

## Freelance writer, agency or writing platform: what budget to plan

The choice of provider directly affects the budget to plan. A **freelance writer** generally offers more accessible rates than an agency, since they do not carry structural costs or an intermediary margin: this is a fitting option for a moderate production volume, with a single point of contact and a trust based relationship built over time.

A **web writing agency** in turn brings full project management, a range of writing profiles and the capacity to absorb a large volume without depending on a single person's availability. Its rate logically reflects this coordination service, which explains a generally higher cost than an isolated freelancer.

**Writing platforms**, for their part, tend to offer rates aligned with the lowest per word billing on the market. They suit occasional needs with low quality requirements, but rarely a [content marketing strategy](/en/blog/content-marketing/) aimed at building a lasting audience and a consistent [editorial line](/en/blog/editorial-line/) over time.

Whatever provider is chosen, the quality of the final text also depends on mastering [copywriting techniques](/en/blog/copywriting-techniques/) and [web writing](/en/blog/web-writing/) best practices, criteria worth checking before comparing prices alone.

Before comparing quotes, it remains useful to clarify internally what the expected service actually covers. A low rate can hide a text delivered without keyword research or SEO structuring, while a higher rate often includes a briefing phase, proofreading and sometimes one round of revisions after initial feedback. Comparing prices without comparing the actual content of the service regularly leads to disappointment, on the client side as much as on the writer's side.

The volume of content to produce also changes the equation. A company publishing a one off article does not have the same needs as a brand feeding a regular [editorial calendar](/en/blog/editorial-calendar/) over several months: in that second case, an ongoing relationship with the same writer or agency generally helps build consistency of tone, at the cost of a longer commitment than for a single order.
