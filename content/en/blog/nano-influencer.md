A **nano-influencer** is a content creator whose community stays deliberately small, most often between 1,000 and 10,000 followers depending on the definition used. Far from being a mere diminutive of the micro-influencer, this profile has become established in influencer marketing strategies for its ability to generate strong engagement with a niche audience, at a cost far lower than that of more followed creators.

## What is a nano-influencer

A nano-influencer is a content creator active on social media whose audience is counted in thousands of followers rather than tens or hundreds of thousands. Definitions vary by source: some place the floor at 1,000 followers, others use a lower cap of 3,000, with most agreeing on an upper limit around 10,000 followers before moving into the micro-influencer category.

What truly defines a nano-influencer is not so much this threshold, which shifts from one source to another, but the nature of the relationship they maintain with their community. They are most often an individual who shares **niche** content, built around a passion, a profession or a specific lifestyle, without initially aiming for a professional creator career. This spontaneous, low-production format often brings nano-influencer content close to [UGC](/en/blog/ugc-marketing/), even when a financial arrangement governs the post.

## Nano-influencer vs micro-influencer: what are the differences

The boundary between nano and micro-influencer mainly lies in audience size, with the latter generally starting where the former ends, around 10,000 followers. Beyond this threshold, the dynamic of the relationship with the community gradually changes.

Nano-influencers generally retain the ability to respond individually to a large share of the comments and messages they receive, which fosters a sense of closeness that is hard to reproduce at a larger scale. Micro-influencers, by reaching a wider audience, gain reach but mechanically lose part of this direct interaction.

This difference explains why the two profiles do not serve the same marketing objective. A nano-influencer suits a brand looking to anchor itself in a specific community and build trust, while a micro-influencer allows for wider distribution while keeping an engagement level above that of a macro-influencer or a celebrity.

The bigger the audience grows, from nano to micro then macro-influencer, the more the relationship with the community tends to professionalize and move away from a direct peer-to-peer exchange. A macro-influencer most often manages partnerships through an agency or manager, while a nano-influencer negotiates directly with the brand, which simplifies exchanges but also means the brand itself has to handle the collaboration's follow-up, from brief to publication.

## Why brands rely on nano-influencers

Despite a small audience, nano-influencers show some of the highest engagement rates in the **influencer marketing** ecosystem. This performance stems from the nature of the relationship they maintain with their followers, built on a closeness perceived as more authentic than with a creator followed by hundreds of thousands of people.

A nano-influencer's recommendations are generally perceived as less commercial and more personal, which strengthens their impact on the purchase decisions of a niche audience. A brand that collaborates with several nano-influencers at once can thus cover very targeted audience segments, at a combined cost often lower than that of a single collaboration with a macro-influencer.

On TikTok, where the [algorithm](/en/blog/tiktok-algorithm/) rewards native, low-production content more than the size of a creator's starting audience, an authentic-sounding nano-influencer can achieve a one-off reach well beyond their follower count, which further strengthens the appeal of this format for brands.

The reduced entry cost also allows brands to test an influencer approach without committing a large budget, which makes this format particularly suited to small structures and product launches targeted at a specific niche.

## What rate to expect for a nano-influencer collaboration

On Instagram, a sponsored post published by a nano-influencer is most often negotiated between 50 and 150 euros, with a story usually sitting at the lower end of that range. These figures remain indicative: there is no official rate card in the industry, and significant gaps can be observed between two profiles with otherwise comparable audiences.

Several factors push this rate upward. A profitable industry, such as personal finance or B2B, often allows for higher rates than a generalist lifestyle niche. The requested format also matters: an elaborate short video generally costs more than a simple story, and an exclusivity clause, which prevents the creator from working with competing brands for a set period, can significantly raise the negotiated amount.

Flat-fee payment per publication remains the dominant model, though some brands prefer product-based compensation rather than cash for a first collaboration, before moving to financial payment if the partnership continues.

Negotiating on price alone remains an incomplete approach. The number of posts included, the brand's right to reuse the content in paid advertising, and the expected delivery timeline deserve to be settled in the first exchange, rather than discovered upon delivery. A written agreement, even in the form of a detailed message exchange, protects both parties and avoids frequent misunderstandings about what the collaboration actually covers.

## How to find and select relevant nano-influencers

Spotting relevant nano-influencers most often goes through dedicated matchmaking platforms, which allow filtering profiles by topic, geographic area and engagement level rather than by follower count alone. These tools centralize profiles that are not necessarily visible through a standard social media search.

Manual research remains useful nonetheless, particularly through niche hashtags or mentions of comparable products already posted by engaged followers of a competing brand. This method takes more time but sometimes uncovers profiles that are not yet heavily solicited, before they become targets sought after by the whole industry.

At the selection stage, a profile's actual engagement rate, calculated on its most recent posts rather than an old average, matters more than the raw follower count. A creator whose community precisely matches the brand's [persona](/en/blog/create-marketing-persona/), even with a modest audience, generally produces better results than a more followed but less targeted profile.

Compliance of the collaboration with [French influencer law](/en/blog/influencer-law-france/) deserves to be checked at this stage: the explicit mention of a commercial partnership applies to nano-influencers just as to any paid creator, regardless of audience size.

Tracking the actual performance of a campaign run with several nano-influencers requires going beyond simply counting mentions or likes. Assigning a distinct promo code or link to each creator makes it possible to objectively compare their respective contribution, rather than judging the collaboration on the general impression left by the post alone. This tracking discipline, often neglected for modest budgets, is what separates brands that keep renewing partnerships with their best-performing nano-influencers from those that spread their budget without ever identifying the profiles that actually convert.
